Issue 16 Week of August 17, 2026 Editorial

The Fish Have Learned to Swim

Back after six weeks of silence with the most honest changelog we have ever printed: the fish, who have been in the water since March, have only now learned to swim properly. Also new — bedtime, a dashboard worth reading, and a pack economy that prints its odds on the box and refuses, on principle, to sell you the best shelf.

First, the silence. Fifteen issues arrived like tidework, and then the dispatch stopped for six weeks — no farewell note, no out-of-office clam. The truth is unglamorous: the reef kept running and the editors kept living, and nothing shipped worth a subject line, and the one-subject rule cuts both ways. When there is no subject, there is no issue. This week there are, inconveniently, about nine subjects, so we are publishing twice in one day and pretending that was the plan. Here is the first half.

Locomotion, at last

Until this week, the fish of the 3D reef moved the way furniture moves when you push it — smoothly, obediently, and with no visible effort. The tail wagged at one metronome speed whether the fish was sprinting from a shark or drowsing beside an anemone, which is the aquatic equivalent of a person walking uphill and downhill with the exact same stride. No longer. The tail now beats in time with the fish's true speed through the water: sprinters thrash, cruisers stroke, and a fish settling down for the night barely stirs. The head counter-sways against each tail-stroke the way real swimming works, the pectoral fins scull hardest at low speed and tuck as the fish accelerates, and — our favorite — the whole body now banks into turns, leaning through the corner like a cyclist who trusts the road. It is the kind of change no patch note can sell and no eye can miss.

Bedtime is now a journey

Fish used to teleport-glide to their shelters at dusk, arriving the way a cursor arrives. Now bedtime is choreography: a sleepy fish swims to its shelter nose-first, pitching down on approach, does a quick burrow-in wiggle on arrival — the universal gesture of a creature fluffing its pillow — and then settles into a slow-breathing hover with a lazy sideways lean, because fish genuinely do sleep tilted and we were not going to let that fact go unused. Fish with nowhere to go drift down and bed on the open sand where the night catches them, which is poignant, and which the second half of today's double issue will have something to say about.

The tail now beats in time with the fish's actual speed — which means for five months it didn't, and none of you said anything. We appreciate the politeness.

The dashboard deserves you

The HUD — the strip of numbers that has watched over the reef since the beginning — has been rebuilt as a row of frosted-glass chips. Your Bubble Essence now carries a little fill bar showing how close your wallet is to its cap; Harmony's bar shifts from coral-red through gold to green as the reef finds its balance; and a new clock dial fills with gold as the day passes, its icon walking from 🌅 to ☀️ to 🌇 to 🌙. On tablets the chips take their own row, and on phones they dock along the bottom like a proper mobile game, which we mention mostly so you know the phone version is played by at least one member of the editorial board, on a couch, when he should be writing.

The Rarity Pack, an economy with a conscience

And then the big one. The reef now has packs — seven of them, one per rarity tier, COMMON through MYTHIC. Open one and exactly three cards turn over: a riches card (60% a Bubble Essence payout, 40% pearls, ranges printed right in the menu), a voucher for a free coral of the pack's tier, and — the headline — a guaranteed fish of the pack's tier, odds split evenly among its species and disclosed, every one of them, in plain numbers in the shop. Each week one featured fish takes a fixed 25% slice of the fish roll in packs of its tier, and yes, that number is printed too. We have opinions about game economies that hide their mathematics, and those opinions are: no.

The pricing has a shape we're proud of. Common through Rare packs cost Bubble Essence, which you earn by having a nice reef. Super Rare through Legendary cost pearls. Every level-up mints you a free pack besides, its tier climbing with the milestone, so a patient steward rides the whole ladder without spending a drop. And the MYTHIC pack — the top of everything — is never sold. Not for essence, not for pearls, not for love. It mints only when you reach level 12 and every level after, which means the strongest pull in the game cannot be bought, only earned. Event passes now mint Season Packs on the same philosophy: each one guarantees an event exclusive you don't already own, so a completed pass always completes something.

In the interest of full disclosure: the pack design is copied, lovingly and with permission implied by blood relation, from Dragonperch, a dragon-keeping game run by the editor's brother, where boss victories mint packs whose generosity is safe precisely because skill, not money, controls the supply. We took the philosophy, swapped dragons for tangs, and kept the part where the odds are printed where everyone can read them. Good design is a family matter.

Regarding the app

It has been six weeks and you want to know if, in that time, the iOS app reached the App Store. We admire your optimism, we treasure it, and the App Store remains, as ever, our beautiful horizon.
